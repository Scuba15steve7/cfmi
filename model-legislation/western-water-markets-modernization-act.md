## Free-Market Impact Statement

**Bill:** Western Water Markets Modernization and Property Rights Act  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Editor:** CFMI bootstrap

### One-sentence market claim

This Act clarifies property in conserved water, lowers transaction costs for voluntary transfers and private banking, and uses limited, rules-based state incentives—without subsidies for favored users, exclusive franchises, or restrictions on a right-holder’s current lawful use.

### Status quo distortion

| Distortion type | Present? | Brief evidence |
|-----------------|----------|----------------|
| Incumbent subsidy | Often | Preferential project water and opaque allocation favor connected users in many basins |
| Entry / transfer barrier | Yes | Slow, discretionary transfer approval and “forfeiture by conservation” chill trades and efficiency |
| Opaque discretion | Yes | “Public interest” findings without objective criteria delay or deny voluntary deals |
| Legal privilege | Sometimes | Preferential access to conveyance or storage not open on equal published terms |
| Other | Yes | Fear that unused or conserved water will be lost under beneficial-use doctrines deters markets |

### What this proposal does to competition

- **Lowers barriers:** Streamlined timelines and objective criteria for voluntary transfers; facilitation of private banking and market platforms.
- **Clarifies property / contract:** Conserved water meeting objective baselines is protected from forfeiture and is transferable under state law.
- **Leaves unchanged:** Existing priority systems; no forced reallocation; no mandate to sell or fallow.
- **Risks new rents (mitigated):** Incentive payments are opt-in, formula-based, capped, and publicly logged; no named corporate beneficiaries.

### Winners and losers (economic)

Right-holders who conserve or transfer gain clearer title and lower deal costs. Buyers (cities, farms, industry, environmental buyers paying voluntarily) face a thicker market. Intermediaries compete on open platforms rather than political access. Taxpayers avoid open-ended subsidy programs; limited incentives are conditional on measurable market and supply outcomes.

### Instruments used (least → more coercive)

1. Rights clarification (conserved water; no restriction on current use)  
2. Procedural streamlining for voluntary transfers  
3. Transparency mandates (transfer registry, incentive ledger)  
4. Rules-based, opt-in incentives for market depth and new supply  
5. Limited, objective eligibility for certain federal-program coordination where the state opts in  
6. No commandeering; strict non-preemption of stricter private property protections

### Anti-capture checklist

- [x] No named private beneficiary class without open, objective eligibility  
- [x] No perpetual subsidy  
- [x] No exclusive franchise or licensing cartel  
- [x] Approvals use published objective criteria  
- [x] Exit from voluntary programs is permitted  

### Bottom line

Western water scarcity is worsened when conservation is punished, transfers are slow and discretionary, and allocation runs through politics. This Act makes voluntary markets work better by defining conserved water as property, speeding lawful trades, enabling private banking, protecting farmers’ current use, and tying any public incentive to transparent, population-driven demand met through markets and new supply—not through picking winners.

## Constitutional Basis Statement

**Jurisdiction:** State model act (primary). Optional federal coordination provisions are non-commandeering and non-preemptive.

### One-sentence constitutional claim

Water allocation and real-property-adjacent water rights are traditional state domain; this Act exercises state authority to define and protect property, police fraud and third-party injury through objective rules, and may voluntarily coordinate with federal programs without surrendering state control or commandeering sister states.

### Source of authority

| Level | Authority invoked | Notes |
|-------|-------------------|-------|
| State | Property, water code, police power | Core of the Act |
| Federal | None required for enactment | Optional coordination only if state elects |
| Compact / interstate | Respected | Act shall not authorize violation of interstate compacts or Supreme Court decrees |

### Federalism design

| Principle | How the text complies |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Anti-commandeering | No directive to Congress or other states; federal references are opt-in |
| Preemption scope | **Strict non-preemption:** Act does not preempt local protections of property that are more protective of right-holders; does not occupy fields reserved to interstate compact |
| Experimentation | States may adopt in whole or in part; definitions allow basin-specific baselines |
| Competitive pressure | Model act invites adoption by demonstration, not federal mandate |

### Property and contract

- Strengthens alienability and security of conserved water meeting objective baselines.  
- Prohibits construing the Act to restrict current lawful use.  
- Transfer denials require written findings under published criteria; provides prompt administrative review.  
- No impairment of existing contracts except by voluntary amendment of the parties.

### Separation of powers

Legislative standards govern conservation baselines, transfer timelines, and incentive formulas. Agency role is application of objective criteria and publication—not open-ended “public interest” policymaking.

### Bottom line

A state may clarify water property and reduce political allocation consistent with due process and existing priority systems. This draft refuses federal commandeering and field preemption, keeps incentives optional and rules-based, and treats transparency as a constitutional culture of limited government—not a new surveillance bureaucracy.

## Unintended Consequences & Safeguards

| Risk | L | S | Mitigation in draft |
|------|---|---|---------------------|
| New rent / privilege | M | H | Opt-in incentives; formula caps; public ledger; equal access to facilitation tools |
| Agency discretion creep | M | H | Objective baselines; mandatory timelines; written findings; no standalone “public interest” veto |
| Harm to small/rural right-holders | M | H | No restriction on current use; rural and temporary-transfer preferences in queue processing; technical assistance must be non-exclusive |
| Third-party physical injury ignored | M | M | Expedited review still requires defined no-injury / mitigation showing under published hydrologic criteria |
| Federal overreach | L | H | Non-preemption; opt-in only; compact savings clause |
| Speculative hoarding | M | M | Banking rules require measurable accounts, reporting, and prohibit sham conservation without baseline proof |
| Market thinness / price spikes | M | M | Transparency of offers; temporary transfer fast-track; no ban on new supply projects meeting ordinary law |

**Residual risks accepted:** Priority conflicts and compact constraints remain; markets cannot create wet water that does not exist. The Act improves allocation and conservation incentives within physical and legal reality.

**Bottom line:** Safeguards focus on objective criteria, farmer use protection, rural/temporary preference in processing, public ledgers, and hard limits on subsidies and preemption—so modernization cannot become a new spoils system.

# Operative Text

## Section 1. Title

This Act shall be known as the “Western Water Markets Modernization and Property Rights Act.”

## Section 2. Findings and Purpose

**(a) Findings.** The Legislature finds that:

1. Secure property rights and low-cost voluntary exchange are the least coercive means of allocating scarce water among competing uses;
2. Uncertainty about whether conserved water may be forfeited, and slow discretionary transfer approval, deter efficiency and lawful trade;
3. Opaque allocation and preferential access create opportunities for favoritism inconsistent with equal protection of property;
4. Population-driven demand is best met through a combination of voluntary transfers, conserved water, and new supply developed under generally applicable law—not through political rationing alone; and
5. Federal agencies should not commandeer state water law; coordination must be voluntary and rules-based.

**(b) Purpose.** The purposes of this Act are to:

1. Protect conserved water that meets objective baselines from forfeiture solely by reason of conservation;
2. Streamline voluntary transfers under objective criteria and enforceable timelines;
3. Facilitate private water banking and market platforms on equal terms;
4. Protect right-holders’ current lawful use from restriction under this Act;
5. Authorize limited, transparent incentives for measurable market depth and new supply; and
6. Mandate radical transparency in transfers, banking, and any incentive payments.

## Section 3. Definitions

For purposes of this Act:

1. **“Baseline consumptive use”** means the objectively quantified consumptive use associated with a water right or contract entitlement, calculated under rules issued pursuant to Section 5, using metered data, remote sensing calibrated to local conditions, crop-duty methods, or other published, reproducible methods—and not using ad hoc negotiation for favored parties.

2. **“Conserved water”** means a quantified reduction in baseline consumptive use, achieved by efficiency improvements, deficit irrigation, crop switching, conveyance loss reduction, or other measures, that is capable of being measured or reliably estimated under Section 5 and that does not injure other lawful right-holders.

3. **“Department”** means the state agency charged with administration of water rights, or such successor as the Legislature designates.

4. **“Private water bank”** means a privately owned or operated accounting and storage arrangement that holds conserved or transferred water for later use or transfer under this Act and applicable law.

5. **“Transfer”** means a voluntary change in place of use, purpose of use, point of diversion, or ownership of a water right or of conserved water, including temporary, long-term, and permanent transfers, and including leases.

6. **“Temporary transfer”** means a transfer with a term of not more than three (3) years, or such shorter maximum as the right itself allows.

7. **“Rural right-holder”** means a holder of a water right primarily used for agricultural production on land outside a Census urbanized area, or as further defined by objective population-density criteria in rule.

8. **“Objective criteria”** means published, generally applicable standards that do not depend on the identity of the applicant except as expressly provided for processing priority under Section 7(f).

9. **“Incentive payment”** means a payment authorized under Section 9.

## Section 4. Protection of Current Use

**(a)** Nothing in this Act shall be construed to require any right-holder to conserve, transfer, fallow, or alter current lawful use.

**(b)** Nothing in this Act authorizes the Department to reduce, condition, or restate a water right solely because the holder declines to participate in markets, banking, or incentive programs.

**(c)** Participation in any program under this Act is voluntary.

## Section 5. Conserved Water — Property Protection

**(a) Qualification.** Conserved water that:

1. Is measured against a baseline consumptive use established under subsection (c);
2. Is achieved by the right-holder or by agreement with the right-holder; and
3. Does not injure other lawful water rights as determined under objective hydrologic criteria,

shall not be subject to abandonment, forfeiture, or cancellation **solely** because of the reduction in diversion or use attributable to that conservation.

**(b) Alienability.** Conserved water qualified under subsection (a) is a usufructuary interest appurtenant to the underlying right (or as otherwise recognized under state property principles) and may be transferred under Section 7, banked under Section 8, or used by the right-holder, consistent with priority and compact obligations.

**(c) Baseline rules.** Within twelve (12) months of the effective date, the Department shall adopt rules establishing:

1. Methods for calculating baseline consumptive use by right class and basin;
2. Documentation standards;
3. Injury analysis methods; and
4. A public comment process.

Baselines shall be **reproducible** and shall not be waived for particular applicants except by a published variance standard available to all similarly situated applicants.

**(d) Sham conservation.** Diversion reductions that merely shift consumptive use to another parcel under common control without net reduction, or that lack documentation under subsection (c), do not qualify.

**(e) No creation of water.** This Section does not authorize diversion or use beyond the underlying right, compact, or physical availability.

## Section 6. Duty of Open Administration

**(a)** The Department shall publish, in machine-readable format where feasible:

1. Pending and completed transfer applications and final actions;
2. Qualified conserved-water quantities and associated rights;
3. Private bank account summaries as required by Section 8 (subject to Section 10 privacy limits);
4. All incentive payments under Section 9; and
5. Mean and median processing times by transfer class.

**(b)** Guidance documents that affect substantive rights shall be published as rules subject to ordinary rulemaking, not as informal memoranda alone.

## Section 7. Streamlined Voluntary Transfers

**(a) Applicability.** This Section applies to voluntary transfers of water rights and of conserved water qualified under Section 5.

**(b) Completeness.** Within twenty (20) days of filing, the Department shall determine whether an application is complete under a published checklist. Incomplete applications shall receive a single itemized deficiency notice. Upon resubmission curing listed deficiencies, the application is complete.

**(c) Timelines.**

1. **Temporary transfers:** Final action within sixty (60) days after completeness, unless a defined injury protest is timely filed.
2. **Other transfers:** Final action within one hundred eighty (180) days after completeness, unless a defined injury protest is timely filed.
3. Failure to act within these periods shall entitle the applicant to petition a court of competent jurisdiction for an order compelling decision within thirty (30) days, without altering substantive standards.

**(d) Approval standard.** The Department shall approve a transfer if:

1. The transfer is voluntary and authorized by the right-holder;
2. The quantity and season are within the right and any conserved-water qualification;
3. The transfer will not injure other lawful water rights under objective hydrologic criteria, or injury is fully mitigated by conditions accepted by the applicant; and
4. The transfer does not violate an interstate compact, Supreme Court decree, or federally reserved right as adjudicated or recognized under law.

**(e) Prohibited grounds.** The Department shall **not** deny or condition a transfer based on:

1. The identity of the buyer or seller, except for lawful sanctions against fraud;
2. Preference for a particular economic sector as such;
3. A generalized “public interest,” “socioeconomic,” or “community” finding not reduced to injury, compact, or fraud criteria published in rule; or
4. The applicant’s nonparticipation in unrelated Department programs.

**(f) Processing preference (not substantive preference).** In ordering its workload when applications are backlogged, the Department shall prioritize complete applications for:

1. Temporary transfers; then
2. Transfers involving at least one rural right-holder as transferor; then
3. Other transfers,

in chronological order within each class. This subsection does not alter the approval standard in subsection (d).

**(g) Protests.** Standing to protest shall be limited to holders of water rights or compact interests that allege specific injury under objective criteria, and to the Attorney General as to compact compliance. Protest fees shall not exceed actual average processing cost.

**(h) Conveyance access.** Where the State or a political subdivision controls conveyance capacity not fully committed by contract, access rules shall be published, non-discriminatory among lawful water, and priced (if at all) by a schedule of general application—not by case-by-case favor.

## Section 8. Private Water Banking Facilitation

**(a)** The Department shall establish a registration system for private water banks that:

1. Requires transparent accounting of deposits, withdrawals, losses, and ownership;
2. Requires annual public reports of aggregate balances by basin; and
3. Charges fees limited to average administrative cost.

**(b)** Registration shall be available to any person meeting objective solvency and accounting standards. The Department shall not limit the number of banks by franchise cap or exclusive license.

**(c)** Banked conserved water retains priority characteristics required by underlying law; banking does not reset priority or create a superior right.

**(d)** The State may operate a public accounting platform for notice and clearing **only** as a non-exclusive facilitation tool. Any public platform shall interoperate via open data standards and shall not mandate use as a condition of transfer approval under Section 7.

**(e)** Storage in private or public facilities remains subject to contract and generally applicable safety and environmental law; this Act creates no entitlement to subsidized storage.

## Section 9. State Incentives for Market Depth and New Supply

**(a) Authorization.** Subject to appropriation, the State may provide **opt-in** incentive payments solely to:

1. Reduce documented transaction costs for small and rural transferors (e.g., metering, legal description, baseline quantification); or
2. Support new water supply projects that sell water through open, non-discriminatory contracts and that meet ordinary permitting law,

provided the payments follow a published formula and do not guarantee profit.

**(b) Population-driven demand metric.** Before issuing payments under subsection (a)(2), the Department shall publish a basin-level estimate of population-driven demand growth and the share met in the prior three years by (i) voluntary transfers and conserved water, and (ii) new supply. Incentive priority shall favor basins where markets and new supply demonstrably meet a larger share of that growth relative to administrative rationing.

**(c) Caps and sunlight.**

1. No single recipient may receive more than a published annual percentage of the program appropriation (default: ten percent (10%) unless the Legislature sets another figure).
2. All payments shall be posted within thirty (30) days with recipient, amount, basin, and statutory basis.
3. Incentives shall sunset five (5) years after first payment unless reauthorized after a public report on market thickness and rent risks.

**(d) Prohibitions.** Incentive payments shall not:

1. Pay for political advocacy;
2. Prefer a named corporation or nonprofit by special legislation;
3. Condition payment on surrender of speech or on exclusive dealing with a state platform; or
4. Function as a perpetual operating subsidy for water users.

**(e) Farmer protection.** No incentive program under this Section may require fallowing as a condition of retaining an existing right; fallowing may only be consideration in a voluntary contract.

## Section 10. Transparency; Privacy Limits

**(a)** Transfer prices may be reported as ranges if necessary to protect legitimate trade secrets, provided volume, term, basin, and approval status are public.

**(b)** Personal contact information of individual small right-holders shall not be sold by the State.

**(c)** Source code or spreadsheet methods used for baseline calculation in public decisions shall be published or described with sufficient detail for independent replication.

## Section 11. Limited Federal Coordination (Non-Commandeering)

**(a)** The Governor or Department may elect to coordinate with federal agencies regarding project water or federal facilities **only** under written agreements that:

1. Acknowledge state ownership and administration of water rights under state law;
2. Use objective scheduling and accounting criteria; and
3. Are published in full.

**(b)** No provision of this Act shall be construed to authorize federal commandeering of state officers, or to condition core water-right administration on adoption of open-ended federal discretionary programs.

**(c)** If federal law offers conditional funding for market facilitation consistent with Sections 5–9, the State may accept only with public disclosure of conditions and a finding that conditions do not create artificial barriers or named private privileges.

## Section 12. Strict Non-Preemption; Savings

**(a)** This Act shall not be construed to preempt local or state provisions that provide **stronger** protection for water right security, conserved-water title, or transfer timelines, except where dual compliance is impossible, in which case the provision more protective of voluntary exchange and clear title controls as to that conflict.

**(b)** This Act does not impair interstate compacts, equitable apportionment decrees, tribal water rights, or federally reserved rights.

**(c)** This Act does not expand surface or groundwater withdrawal beyond existing authorized rights.

## Section 13. Judicial Review

Final Department actions under Sections 5, 7, 8, or 9 shall be subject to judicial review under the state administrative procedure act. Courts shall review factual injury findings for substantial evidence and shall review legal interpretations of this Act de novo.

## Section 14. Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application is held invalid, the remainder shall remain in force.

## Section 15. Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the calendar year following enactment, except that rulemaking authority under Sections 5 and 7 is effective immediately.
