# Issue Brief: Certificate of Need (Healthcare Facility Barriers)

**Status:** Priority example · research brief (not a scored bill)  
**Domain:** State health-facility regulation  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Problem (plain language)

In Certificate of Need (CON) states, hospitals, ambulatory surgery centers, imaging facilities, and sometimes nursing homes must get permission from a state board **before competing**—even if they have capital, staff, and willing patients. Incumbents often oppose new capacity. Patients face less choice and higher prices; entrepreneurs cannot simply enter.

## How government is the bottleneck

CON is an explicit **legal barrier to entry** justified historically as cost control. In practice it protects existing facilities from competition. The market is ready to supply more beds, OR time, or scanners; statute makes that supply illegal without political approval. That is government allocation of capacity, not open competition.

## Why it fits the Charter

| Principle | Fit |
|-----------|-----|
| **Free markets** | Entry is political, not price-and-quality driven. |
| **No artificial manipulation** | Legal privileges and exclusive capacity rights for incumbents. |
| **Rent / corruption surface** | Hearings and “need” findings invite lobbying over competition. |
| **Minimal intervention** | Repeal or narrow CON is less coercive than managing statewide bed counts. |
| **Competitive federalism** | States that repeal CON provide a living experiment; federal commandeering is not required. |

## What a CFMI-style AI product looks like

1. **Score** repeal, “modernization,” and expansion bills (watch for modernization that keeps the gate).  
2. **Publish conflicts** when “need,” “duplication,” or incumbent-notification rights block free entry.  
3. **Open fix language** — full repeal; or sunset + objective safety licensing only (no economic-need test).  
4. **Sample act** for bipartisan comment: CON repeal with residual facility safety standards kept separate from economic entry control.

## Hooks to flag (statutory / regulatory language)

- “No person shall construct, expand, or offer [service] without a certificate of need…”  
- Approval factors such as “unnecessary duplication,” “impact on existing providers,” or “community need” without objective, published metrics.  
- Standing for incumbent providers to contest applications; fee structures that fund opposition theater.  
- Moratoria on new facilities or equipment by decree of the same board that licenses competitors.
