# Issue Brief: Exclusive Franchises and Closed AI / Tech Market Rules

**Status:** Priority example · research brief (not a scored bill)  
**Domain:** State and local utility/franchise law; emerging state/federal AI and digital-market rules  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Issue description

Two related patterns block open competition. First, exclusive franchises—electric, gas, cable, sometimes waste or transit—give one firm a legal monopoly over a territory; rivals cannot offer service even at a better price. Second, new AI and digital-market rules can harden the same logic: licensing regimes, procurement that names incumbents, liability shields available only to approved providers, or standards drafted as entry barriers. Progress stalls behind permission, not behind a lack of ideas.

## How government blocks free-market solutions

Exclusive franchises are government-created monopolies. In AI and adjacent tech markets, the risk is repeating that playbook: treating computational services, models, or platforms as utilities to be licensed and sheltered rather than as competitive industries under equal rules of property, contract, and tort. Free-market solutions (open entry, portable standards, voluntary certification) get crowded out when statute picks winners.

## CFMI proposed approach

Educational and research work only—not whipping votes or advising any person how to act:

1. **Score** franchise, utility, and AI-governance bills for entry barriers and named privileges.  
2. **Publish conflicts** when “safety” language is a vehicle for incumbent shelter or opaque licensing.  
3. **Offer open fix / alignment language** — competitive entry, equal liability rules, transparent procurement, sunset on emergency powers.  
4. **Circulate sample model text** (queued: AI markets open competition) for bipartisan comment—barrier reduction, not a subsidy race.

## Success looks like

Utility and tech markets default to open entry under equal rules; exclusive-franchise expansions and AI licensing cartels are scored in public with open competitive-entry language for bipartisan comment.

---

### Charter fit (summary)

Legal monopolies and exclusive franchises named in the Charter; no insulating privileges; competitive federalism; anti-capture (no rents for favored AI firms or utilities).

### Hooks to flag

- “Exclusive franchise,” “sole provider,” or territorial bans on competitive service.  
- AI or compute “licenses to operate” with board discretion and incumbent-heavy composition.  
- Liability shields, safe harbors, or procurement set-asides limited to pre-approved vendors.  
- Standards that incorporate a private consortium’s specs as the only lawful compliance path without equal access for rivals.
