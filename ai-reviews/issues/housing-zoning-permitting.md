# Issue Brief: Housing Zoning and Permitting Barriers

**Status:** Priority example · research brief (not a scored bill)  
**Domain:** Local and state land-use law; federal funding conditions sometimes amplify  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Problem (plain language)

In many metro areas, housing costs outpace wages because supply cannot legally respond. Builders and owners face zoning that bans apartments, duplexes, or ADUs on most lots; parking and design mandates; and permitting timelines measured in years. Willing buyers and sellers exist—the rulebook blocks the deal.

## How government is the bottleneck

Scarcity here is largely **artificial**: zoning maps, discretionary design review, and sequential permits that can kill projects after sunk cost. That is not a free-market failure of “builders refusing to build”; it is political control of density, use, and process. Market pricing would expand supply where demand is high if law allowed open entry into residential construction at scale.

## Why it fits the Charter

| Principle | Fit |
|-----------|-----|
| **Free markets** | Political allocation of buildable density replaces voluntary exchange. |
| **Property & contract** | Use restrictions and process risk weaken the ability to put land to productive housing use. |
| **No artificial manipulation** | Incumbent homeowners and favored developers gain from barriers that raise rivals’ costs. |
| **Opaque discretion** | “Compatibility,” “character,” and case-by-case waivers without objective criteria are Hard-Flag territory. |
| **Minimal intervention** | Clarifying by-right uses and time-bound permits often beats new housing bureaucracies. |

## What a CFMI-style AI product looks like

1. **Score** state/local housing bills and zoning reform packages for barrier reduction vs. new discretionary layers.  
2. **Publish conflicts** when “affordable housing” vehicles create named privileges, opaque waivers, or incumbent carve-outs.  
3. **Fix language** — by-right missing-middle housing, shot clocks, published objective standards, limited preemption that leaves state experimentation room (no commandeering).  
4. **Sample act** for bipartisan comment (queued: housing permitting modernization).

## Hooks to flag (statutory / regulatory language)

- Use tables that ban multifamily or ADUs on the vast majority of residential lots without an objective upzoning path.  
- Discretionary “special permit / conditional use / design review” with standards like “harmony with neighborhood character.”  
- Permit timelines with no deemed-approved shot clock, or fees that fund delay rather than cost recovery.  
- Inclusionary or density-bonus schemes that trade political favors for project-specific waivers unavailable on equal, published terms.
