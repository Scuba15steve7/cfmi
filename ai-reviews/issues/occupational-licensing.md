# Issue Brief: Occupational Licensing Barriers

**Status:** Priority example · research brief (not a scored bill)  
**Domain:** State (primarily) · some federal spillover via accreditation and interstate practice  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Issue description

Many people cannot lawfully offer ordinary services—hair braiding, home remodeling, interior design, aspects of healthcare, and dozens of other trades—until they complete hundreds or thousands of hours of schooling, pay fees, and obtain a state license. Consumers pay higher prices; workers with skill but without the approved credential stay out. Safety-sensitive fields need clear rules; much licensing goes far beyond that.

## How government blocks free-market solutions

The barrier is not “the market refusing to compete.” Statute and board regulation make open entry illegal without a government permission slip. Boards are often dominated by incumbents who set hour requirements, exam content, and scope lines that raise rivals’ costs. Voluntary certification, private insurance, and liability rules can signal quality without a political gate—but law crowds those options out when a license is the only lawful path.

## CFMI proposed approach

Educational and research work only—not whipping votes or advising any person how to act:

1. **Score living bills** that expand or “modernize” licensing (new titles, more hours, title protection, interstate compact language that entrenches boards).  
2. **Publish conflicts** where text hardens barriers, expands board discretion, or creates transferable privileges for licensees.  
3. **Offer open fix / alignment language** — sunsets, hour caps, right-to-earn defaults, recognition of out-of-state licenses with objective criteria.  
4. **Circulate sample model text** for bipartisan review and comment (queued: occupational licensing reform)—least-coercive, anti-capture, no new rents.

## Success looks like

States compete to lower artificial entry barriers while keeping narrow, evidence-based safety rules. CFMI’s published scores and open fix language make rent-seeking licensing expansions easy to spot before they harden into law.

---

### Charter fit (summary)

Free markets and open entry; opposition to barriers that raise rivals’ costs; least-coercive tools first; competitive federalism (no federal commandeering of state boards).

### Hooks to flag

- “No person shall engage in [occupation] without a license issued by the Board…”  
- Hour or credit mandates far above demonstrated safety need; “substantially equivalent” left undefined.  
- Board composition requiring a majority of current licensees with no public-member majority or sunset.  
- Vague “public interest,” “moral character,” or “as the Board deems appropriate” denial authority without published objective criteria.
