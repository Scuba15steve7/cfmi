# Issue Brief: Occupational Licensing Barriers

**Status:** Priority example · research brief (not a scored bill)  
**Domain:** State (primarily) · some federal spillover via accreditation and interstate practice  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Problem (plain language)

Many people cannot lawfully offer ordinary services—hair braiding, home remodeling, interior design, aspects of healthcare, and dozens of other trades—until they complete hundreds or thousands of hours of schooling, pay fees, and obtain a state license. Consumers pay higher prices; workers with skill but without the approved credential stay out. Safety-sensitive fields need clear rules; much licensing goes far beyond that.

## How government is the bottleneck

The barrier is not “the market refusing to compete.” It is **statute and board regulation** that make open entry illegal without a government permission slip. Licensing boards are often dominated by incumbents who set hour requirements, exam content, and scope lines that raise rivals’ costs. Voluntary certification, private insurance, and liability rules can signal quality without a political gate.

## Why it fits the Charter

| Principle | Fit |
|-----------|-----|
| **Free markets** | Prices and open entry are displaced by political allocation of who may practice. |
| **No artificial manipulation** | Licensing designed primarily to raise rivals’ costs is a classic entry barrier. |
| **Minimal intervention** | Least-coercive tools (disclosure, voluntary certification, targeted safety rules) often beat broad occupational cartels. |
| **Competitive federalism** | States can compete by lowering artificial barriers; federal commandeering of state boards is not the fix. |

## What a CFMI-style AI product looks like

1. **Score living bills** that expand or “modernize” licensing (new titles, more hours, title protection, interstate compact language that entrench boards).  
2. **Publish conflicts** where text hardens barriers, expands board discretion, or creates transferable privileges for licensees.  
3. **Open fix language** — sunsets, hour caps, right-to-earn defaults, recognition of out-of-state licenses with objective criteria.  
4. **Sample act** for bipartisan comment (queued: occupational licensing reform) — least-coercive, anti-capture, no new rents.

## Hooks to flag (statutory / regulatory language)

- “No person shall engage in [occupation] without a license issued by the Board…”  
- Hour or credit mandates far above demonstrated safety need; “substantially equivalent” left undefined.  
- Board composition requiring a majority of current licensees with no public-member majority or sunset.  
- Vague “public interest,” “moral character,” or “as the Board deems appropriate” denial authority without published objective criteria.
