# Issue Brief: Accreditation and Credential Gatekeeping

**Status:** Priority example · research brief (not a scored bill)  
**Domain:** Federal student-aid eligibility rules; state professional boards; private accreditors with public gatekeeper power  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Problem (plain language)

Access to many careers and to federal student aid runs through **accreditation and board-approved schools**. When a small set of accreditors or professional associations effectively control who may train, sit for exams, or receive aid, alternatives—apprenticeships, competency exams, new schools—struggle even when quality is demonstrable. Students and workers face higher costs and fewer paths.

## How government is the bottleneck

Private associations become cartels when **government outsources the gate**: only accredited programs count for licensure or Title IV aid; boards require graduation from association-approved schools. That is not consumers freely choosing seals of quality; it is law converting a private standard into a legal monopoly over entry. Voluntary private accreditation without exclusive legal privilege is different.

## Why it fits the Charter

| Principle | Fit |
|-----------|-----|
| **No artificial manipulation** | Standards used primarily to raise rivals’ costs or lock in incumbents. |
| **Free markets** | Open entry into training and practice is constrained by political-legal privilege. |
| **Anti-capture** | Gatekeepers with transferable control over who may compete invite capture. |
| **Minimal intervention** | Prefer multiple paths (exam-only, apprenticeship, plural accreditors) over a single mandatory pipeline. |
| **Constitutional stakes (federal)** | Conditions on spending should be clear and objective—not open-ended deference to a closed guild. |

## What a CFMI-style AI product looks like

1. **Score** higher-ed, workforce, and scope-of-practice bills that harden single-pipeline requirements.  
2. **Publish conflicts** when accreditation is the only lawful path and alternatives are barred without safety evidence.  
3. **Open fix language** — plural recognition, competency-based entry, sunlight on board–association overlaps.  
4. **Sample act** for bipartisan comment: alternate pathways to licensure and aid eligibility with objective quality metrics.

## Hooks to flag (statutory / regulatory language)

- “Graduation from a program accredited by [named association]” as the sole eligibility criterion.  
- Exclusive federal recognition of one or few accreditors with no comparable competitive path.  
- Scope statutes that ban supervised practice or exam-only routes used successfully elsewhere.  
- Delegation of binding standards to private bodies without published criteria, appeal, or equal access for new entrants.
