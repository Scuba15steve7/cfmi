# Issue Brief: Incumbent Subsidies and Industrial Privileges

**Status:** Priority example · research brief (not a scored bill)  
**Domain:** Federal and state tax, grant, loan-guarantee, and procurement law  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Issue description

Large, durable subsidies, tax preferences, loan guarantees, and “strategic” industrial packages steer capital toward politically favored firms and sectors. Competitors without a Washington or statehouse relationship face a tilted field. Consumers and taxpayers fund the privilege; innovation that does not fit the program lags.

## How government blocks free-market solutions

The obstacle is not missing demand or private capital alone—it is government-guaranteed demand, preferential tax treatment, and named or class-based privileges that displace open competition. Free markets coordinate through prices; industrial privilege coordinates through political access. Even well-intended “competitiveness” bills can entrench incumbents.

## CFMI proposed approach

Educational and research work only—not whipping votes or advising any person how to act:

1. **Score** subsidy, tax-credit, and industrial-strategy bills for rent creation versus neutral rules.  
2. **Publish conflicts** when text picks winners, guarantees offtake, or creates privileges not available on equal terms.  
3. **Offer open fix / alignment language** — sunsets, equal eligibility, published criteria, clawbacks; prefer barrier removal over new spending.  
4. **Circulate sample model text** when warranted: subsidy sunlight and equal-treatment rules—not a counter-subsidy for a different faction.

## Success looks like

Industrial and tax packages are scored against Charter anti-rent filters; open fix language narrows or sunsets privileges, and sample sunlight rules invite bipartisan comment without CFMI claiming a vote whip.

---

### Charter fit (summary)

Artificial manipulation via incumbent subsidies and guaranteed demand; anti-capture; free markets over political allocation; silence or repeal often beats a new program.

### Hooks to flag

- Named beneficiaries, plant-specific earmarks, or “eligible entity” definitions that map to a short incumbent list.  
- Loan guarantees, contracts-for-difference, or offtake mandates that socialize downside for favored firms.  
- Tax credits stackable only for projects pre-cleared by discretionary agencies.  
- “As the Secretary determines appropriate” selection authority without published, objective scoring.
