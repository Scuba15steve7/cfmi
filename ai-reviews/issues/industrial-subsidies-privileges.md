# Issue Brief: Incumbent Subsidies and Industrial Privileges

**Status:** Priority example · research brief (not a scored bill)  
**Domain:** Federal and state tax, grant, loan-guarantee, and procurement law  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Problem (plain language)

Large, durable subsidies, tax preferences, loan guarantees, and “strategic” industrial packages steer capital toward politically favored firms and sectors. Competitors without a Washington or statehouse relationship face a tilted field. Consumers and taxpayers fund the privilege; innovation that does not fit the program lags.

## How government is the bottleneck

Here the obstacle is not missing demand or private capital alone—it is **government-guaranteed demand, preferential tax treatment, and named or class-based privileges** that displace open competition. Free markets coordinate through prices; industrial privilege coordinates through political access. Even well-intended “competitiveness” bills can entrench incumbents.

## Why it fits the Charter

| Principle | Fit |
|-----------|-----|
| **No artificial manipulation** | Subsidies and guaranteed demand that advantage incumbents are named in the Charter definition. |
| **Anti-capture** | Transferable benefits contingent on political access are refused as CFMI outcomes and flagged in reviews. |
| **Free markets** | Political allocation substitutes for voluntary exchange. |
| **Minimal intervention** | Silence or repeal often beats a new program layered on the last one. |
| **Transparency** | Opaque selection panels and non-public deals are Hard Flags. |

## What a CFMI-style AI product looks like

1. **Score** subsidy, tax-credit, and industrial-strategy bills for rent creation vs. neutral rules.  
2. **Publish conflicts** when text picks winners, guarantees offtake, or creates privileges not available on equal terms.  
3. **Open fix language** — sunsets, equal eligibility, published criteria, clawbacks; prefer barrier removal over new spending.  
4. **Sample act** (when warranted): subsidy sunlight and equal-treatment rules—not a counter-subsidy for a different faction.

## Hooks to flag (statutory / regulatory language)

- Named beneficiaries, plant-specific earmarks, or “eligible entity” definitions that map to a short incumbent list.  
- Loan guarantees, contracts-for-difference, or offtake mandates that socialize downside for favored firms.  
- Tax credits stackable only for projects pre-cleared by discretionary agencies.  
- “As the Secretary determines appropriate” selection authority without published, objective scoring.
