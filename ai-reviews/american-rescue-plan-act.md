## Summary (≤ 120 words)

ARPA authorized roughly **$1.9 trillion** in mandatory COVID-era spending, including the **Coronavirus State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds**, business and household transfers, and numerous grant expansions. Emergency relief can be legitimate; CFMI scores the **durable market-distorting patterns**: large fiscal transfers with broad eligible-use lists, political allocation to favored programs, and weak objective constraints—creating a rent surface and opaque local/federal discretion long after the acute emergency.

## Conflicts with Charter goals

1. **Opaque discretion** in eligible uses and Treasury/agency guidance for fiscal recovery funds.  
2. **Political allocation** of large transfers creates durable constituencies for privilege.  
3. **Artificial manipulation risk** when relief becomes sector industrial policy without equal rules.  
4. **Least coercion inverted** — scale and breadth before narrow, time-limited, published criteria.

## Hard Flags

- [x] Dimension ≤ 1 (E, F, G)
- [x] Artificial manipulation match (opaque discretion / transferable fiscal privilege)

1. **State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds** with broad eligible-use categories refined through guidance.  
2. **Sector and program expansions** allocated by political design rather than equal, published claims.  
3. **Weak sunsets / clawback clarity** relative to the scale of outlays (pattern concern for future vehicles).

## Proposed alignment changes (open fix language for bipartisan comment)

For future emergency vehicles (ARPA is enacted; language is for **alignment templates**):

1. **Narrow eligible uses** to a short, exhaustive list with numerical triggers (e.g., documented public-health outlays; revenue loss measured by a fixed formula).  
2. **Hard sunsets** (e.g., obligation within 12–24 months) and **public, machine-readable** expenditure reporting as a condition of funds.  
3. **Bar** use of emergency fiscal funds for **industrial subsidies, named corporate grants, or new permanent entitlements** without a separate scored bill.  
4. **Equal treatment** — no sector carve-outs unless tied to objective quarantine/closure orders of general application.

## Least-Coercive Alternative Sketch

Time-limited, formulaic fiscal backstops and published public-health procurement; avoid open-ended local industrial policy under emergency labels.
