## Part B — Example Review 1  
### “Domestic Critical Minerals Security and Permitting Acceleration Act” (composite federal pattern)

- **Jurisdiction / session:** U.S. Congress (illustrative composite, 2020s style)
- **Bill ID:** n/a (composite)
- **Review date:** August 2026
- **Human editor:** CFMI bootstrap
- **AI assistance:** drafting aid under rubric 0.1.0; human-edited
- **Interest disclosure (editor):** none

### Summary

The composite bill pairs shortened NEPA-like timelines for “critical mineral” projects with a new grant program for favored processors, a discretionary national-interest waiver for environmental and competition review, and a preference for union-affiliated or “community benefit agreement” counterparties. Speed of permitting can lower entry barriers; the subsidy and waiver architecture reintroduces political allocation and opaque discretion.

### Dimension Scores

| Dim | Score | Hook (section / language) | Note |
|-----|-------|---------------------------|------|
| A. Enumerated / limited power | 2 | Interstate commerce + “national security” findings used to justify grants and waivers nationwide | Security rhetoric without tight limiting principle |
| B. Federalism | 2 | Preempts conflicting state permitting “to the extent inconsistent with timely federal approval” | Soft field pressure on states |
| C. Property & contract | 3 | Faster decisions can secure investment; no clear anti-holdup for existing rights | Mixed |
| D. Free entry & competition | 1 | Grants for “qualified domestic processors”; preference for CBA signatories | Entry tilted to political criteria |
| E. Discretion & opacity | 1 | Secretary may waive reviews if “vital to the national interest” without published metrics | Classic opacity |
| F. Rent / privilege | 1 | Competitive grant program with undefined “strategic partner” priority | Rent surface high |
| G. Least coercion | 2 | Timeline reform is lighter touch; grant/waiver package is heavy | Split bill |
| **Composite** | **1.7** | | |

### Hard Flags

1. **Opaque discretion:** National-interest waiver without objective criteria (Dim E = 1).  
2. **Artificial manipulation:** Processor grants + counterparty preferences create incumbent/political shelter (Dims D, F = 1).  
3. **Preemption pressure:** “Inconsistent with timely federal approval” invites selective displacement of state rules (Dim B = 2, flagged for monitoring).

### Least-Coercive Alternative Sketch

Enact **only** generally applicable permitting shot-clocks and default approvals for complete applications, with injury-based judicial review; publish objective completeness checklists; **omit** sector grants and national-interest waivers. If defense stockpiles are needed, use on-budget procurement with open bidding—not industrial privileges.

### Editor’s Note

Scores accepted. A clean permitting-timeline bill without grants/waivers would likely score ~3.5–4.0 composite.

## Part C — Example Review 2  
### “Affordable Housing Supply and Zoning Accountability Act” (composite federal pattern)

- **Jurisdiction / session:** U.S. Congress (illustrative composite)
- **Bill ID:** n/a (composite)
- **Review date:** August 2026
- **Human editor:** CFMI bootstrap
- **AI assistance:** drafting aid under rubric 0.1.0; human-edited
- **Interest disclosure (editor):** none

### Summary

The composite withholds a portion of surface-transportation formula funds from states that fail to adopt federally specified zoning “accountability” metrics, while authorizing HUD to certify alternate local plans that include inclusionary set-asides and developer fees. Conditional spending can pressure housing supply reform; federally specified land-use content and fee structures risk commandeering-by-spending and new local barriers dressed as affordability.

### Dimension Scores

| Dim | Score | Hook (section / language) | Note |
|-----|-------|---------------------------|------|
| A. Enumerated / limited power | 2 | Spending power + commerce findings to shape local land use | Debatable; historically contested |
| B. Federalism | 1 | Formula-fund withholding unless states enact specified zoning code elements | Coercive federalism pattern |
| C. Property & contract | 2 | Some upzoning potential; inclusionary set-asides and fees burden new supply | Mixed to negative on margins |
| D. Free entry & competition | 2 | Upzoning helps entry; mandatory set-asides/fees raise marginal cost of housing production | Split |
| E. Discretion & opacity | 2 | HUD “alternate plan” certification with multi-factor public-interest list | Discretionary gate |
| F. Rent / privilege | 2 | Fee revenue and set-aside administration create local allocative politics | Medium rent risk |
| G. Least coercion | 1 | Federal fiscal leverage before state competition and information remedies | Heavy instrument first |
| **Composite** | **1.7** | | |

### Hard Flags

1. **Federalism / coercion:** Withholding core formula funds to force zoning content (Dim B = 1, Dim G = 1).  
2. **Barrier risk:** Inclusionary set-asides and fee schedules as conditions of certification can function as entry taxes on new housing (Dim D/F concern).

### Least-Coercive Alternative Sketch

Publish comparable state/local permitting-time and housing-permit data; make federal **competitive** (not formula) planning grants available for states that adopt by-right multifamily in job-rich areas **without** mandatory set-aside/fee templates; avoid conditioning basic transportation formulas on land-use code text. Model state legislation for by-right building is preferable to federal commandeering-by-spending.

### Editor’s Note

Composite accepted. CFMI would separately score a pure transparency-and-by-right model state bill much higher.

## Part D — Example Review 3  
### “Community Bank Resilience and Deposit Stability Act” (composite federal pattern)

- **Jurisdiction / session:** U.S. Congress (illustrative composite)
- **Bill ID:** n/a (composite)
- **Review date:** August 2026
- **Human editor:** CFMI bootstrap
- **AI assistance:** drafting aid under rubric 0.1.0; human-edited
- **Interest disclosure (editor):** none

### Summary

The composite expands discretionary deposit-guarantee coverage upon agency finding of “systemic local stress,” creates a specialized charter pathway with reduced capital for banks that hold a minimum percentage of “community development qualified assets,” and restricts new fintech bank charters pending a multi-year study. Stability language masks a package that privileges incumbents, expands opaque guarantee discretion, and blocks competitive entry.

### Dimension Scores

| Dim | Score | Hook (section / language) | Note |
|-----|-------|---------------------------|------|
| A. Enumerated / limited power | 3 | Banking / necessary-and-proper adjacent to federal fiscal instruments | Familiar but expandable |
| B. Federalism | 3 | Occupies federal guarantee space; leaves state banks partially touched via FDIC interface | Moderate |
| C. Property & contract | 2 | Ad hoc guarantee expansions alter creditor expectations unevenly | Rule-of-law concern |
| D. Free entry & competition | 0 | Moratorium on new fintech-related charters; preferential capital for qualified-asset mix | Entry barrier + shelter |
| E. Discretion & opacity | 1 | Guarantee expansion on “systemic local stress” without numerical triggers | High opacity |
| F. Rent / privilege | 0 | Capital relief tied to politically defined asset categories; charter freeze | Clear privilege |
| G. Least coercion | 1 | Study-plus-moratorium and guarantee discretion before price/ risk transparency | Wrong instrument order |
| **Composite** | **1.4** | | |

### Hard Flags

1. **Entry barrier:** Charter moratorium pending study (Dim D = 0).  
2. **Legal privilege:** Preferential capital treatment for politically qualified assets (Dim F = 0).  
3. **Opaque discretion:** Open-ended guarantee expansion trigger (Dim E = 1).  
4. **Artificial manipulation:** Combined subsidy-equivalent + barrier matches Charter definition.

### Least-Coercive Alternative Sketch

Publish comparable risk metrics and examination manuals; price deposit insurance by risk on transparent schedules of general application; allow new entrants under the **same** capital and liquidity rules as incumbents; avoid asset-category privileges and moratoria. If temporary emergency guarantees are used, require ex ante numerical triggers, short sunsets, and after-action public reports.

### Editor’s Note

Composite accepted. This pattern is a teaching example of anti-Charter financial legislation despite “community” branding.
