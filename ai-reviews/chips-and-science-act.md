## Summary (≤ 120 words)

The CHIPS and Science Act appropriates roughly **$52.7 billion** for semiconductor manufacturing incentives (including about **$39 billion** for fabrication facilities, with authority for loans/loan guarantees) plus a **25% investment tax credit** for semiconductor manufacturing equipment, alongside large science authorizations. National-security and supply-chain findings frame industrial subsidies and discretionary Commerce selection of “eligible” projects. Barrier-reducing science funding is separable; the core incentive architecture is political allocation of capital to favored fabs—classic artificial manipulation under the Charter.

## Conflicts with Charter goals

1. **Incumbent / favored-firm subsidies** — large grants and loan authority allocate capital by political criteria, not open prices.  
2. **Artificial manipulation** — guaranteed public support and tax preferences unavailable on equal terms to all competitors.  
3. **Opaque discretion** — award and agreement negotiation without fully objective, published scoring that would let any qualified entrant claim the same deal.  
4. **Least coercion inverted** — industrial privilege before neutral barrier removal (permitting shot clocks, equal tax treatment).

## Hard Flags

- [x] Dimension ≤ 1 (D, E, F, G)
- [x] Artificial manipulation match (subsidy / privilege)

1. **Subsidy / rent:** ~$39B fabrication incentives and loan/guarantee authority under the CHIPS incentives program (P.L. 117-167 / NDAA §9902 framework).  
2. **Tax privilege:** Advanced manufacturing investment credit for semiconductor manufacturing property (25% credit structure as enacted).  
3. **Discretionary selection:** Secretary of Commerce designation of funded projects and agreement terms.

## Proposed alignment changes (open fix language for bipartisan comment)

Educational amendment sketches—not voting instructions:

1. **Strike or wind down** new CHIPS incentive appropriations and loan-guarantee authority; replace remaining outlays with **on-budget DoD/intel procurement** of specified chips under open competitive bidding.  
2. **Convert ITC** to a short, equal-rate capital-cost recovery schedule available to **all** domestic manufacturing property meeting published, non-sector-exclusive criteria—or repeal and lower generally applicable corporate rates.  
3. **Publish** all award criteria, scores, and agreements in machine-readable form; bar negotiated side-deals not available on the same published schedule.  
4. **Prefer** generally applicable permitting shot clocks and NEPA page/time limits for fabs of any ownership that meet objective completeness checklists—**without** sector grants.

## Least-Coercive Alternative Sketch

Secure critical chips via transparent stockpile procurement and equal rules of entry/permitting; omit industrial privilege packages.
