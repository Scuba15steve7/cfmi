## Executive answer (plain language)

**Why stalled?** Mostly Senate **procedure and partisan math**, not a proven bribery chain. House-passed SAVE vehicles need **60 votes** to clear a filibuster. Senate Democrats are unified against the bills as drafted. Republicans hold a working majority short of 60 and have **not** changed the filibuster rule. A March 2026 cloture-related vote failed **53–47**. As of early August 2026, Majority Leader Thune and conference messaging still say they lack votes to pass the bill or abolish the filibuster.

**Who blocks?** Publicly: the **Democratic caucus** (Leader Schumer and colleagues), citing access/suppression and registration-friction concerns; plus **Republican skeptics** on design/federalism/timing—most clearly Sen. **Lisa Murkowski** (opposes the SAVE America Act as drafted while supporting citizen-only voting and voter ID in principle) and Sen. **Thom Tillis** (calls the floor push a “waste of time,” cites no path to 60 and implementation math). Blocking the *legislative vehicle* is not the same as opposing “only citizens should vote.”

**Corruption / special interests?** Disclosed **advocacy coalitions** lobby hard on both sides (civil-rights / voting-access orgs against; Heritage Action and allied integrity groups for). That is influence, not established payola. **No public-record chain in this pass** shows that a named senator’s “no” was purchased. Treat bribe narratives as **not established**.

**~80% public support?** **Partly true for principles; not established for this bill.** Nonpartisan Gallup/Pew-style questions on *photo ID* and *proof of citizenship for first-time registration* often land ~80–84%. Polls that name the SAVE Act or describe its full package land lower (e.g. Rasmussen ~63% for the SAVE Act, March 2026). Do not equate slogan polls with support for every operative clause.

Reject the slide that “therefore the Senate is defying 80% support for this legislative package.”

## 4. Mechanism of stall (procedure + politics)

### Narrative vs mechanism

| Public narrative | Operative mechanism |
|------------------|---------------------|
| “Corrupt elites kill popular election integrity” | **60-vote cloture** + unified minority opposition + majority short of 60 + majority refusal (so far) to change filibuster rules |
| “One senator is the blocker” | Caucus-level Democratic opposition is the scale factor; Murkowski/Tillis matter at the margin and for *Republican* coalition cohesion, not as the whole story |
| “The people demand this exact bill” | Polls support *principles* more strongly than *this text*; opponents argue operative burdens (in-person docs, mail/online registration friction, DHS matching errors) |
| “Only suppression explains opposition” | Stated reasons also include **federalism**, **rural access**, **implementation timing**, and **false-exclusion risk**—contestable, but public and non-bribe |

### Ranked stall drivers (this pass)

1. **Filibuster / cloture math** — Need ~60; cloture-related vote failed 53–47 (Mar 26, 2026).  
2. **Partisan coalition** — Near-unified Democratic “no” on the vehicles as drafted.  
3. **Majority rules choice** — Thune/conference decline to abolish legislative filibuster; some Republicans defend that institutional choice.  
4. **Intra-GOP design/federalism dissent** — Murkowski (access + state control); Tillis (path-to-60 + implementation timeline); others reported wary of mid-cycle chaos.  
5. **Calendar / leadership sequencing** — Weeks of floor debate without converting to enactment; summer recess fight is pressure politics, not a new vote count.  
6. **Advocacy ecosystem** — Intense FOR/AGAINST lobbying and media campaigns (see §6)—correlated with intensity, **not** established as causation of any one senator’s vote.

**Not established as primary driver in this pass:** Quid-pro-quo corruption; secret NGO “ownership” of Senate Democrats; stolen-election necessity as the legal justification for the bill.

| Allegation style | This pass |
|------------------|-----------|

Label that **interest-group politics**. Do **not** upgrade it to “corruption” without a filing/FEC/indictment chain.

## 7. CFMI assessment

### People-over-bureaucracy / constitutional transparency

- **Support steelman:** Federal elections already require citizenship; attestation-only registration leaves a **verification gap**; published documentary/database pathways can shift power from opaque list quality toward auditable eligibility—if designed with cure rights.  
- **Opposition steelman:** A federal package that collapses mail/online registration, criminalizes official judgment on alternative evidence, or relies on error-prone federal matching can shift power to **bureaucracy and false exclusions**, harming eligible citizens—especially rural, name-change, and document-scarce populations.  
- **Charter lens:** Prefer **transparent verification + least-coercive failsafes** over either “trust attestation forever” or “papers-or-nothing theater.” Competitive federalism: Elections Clause floor for *federal* contests is stronger than commandeering state/local office rules.

### Least-coercive path (open alternative)

See CFMI’s companion design—not a whip for H.R. 22 / S. 1383 as drafted:

- [`issues/federal-election-transparency-package.md`](issues/federal-election-transparency-package.md)  
- Model outline: [`model-legislation/federal-election-transparency-and-audit-standards-act.md`](../model-legislation/federal-election-transparency-and-audit-standards-act.md)

Directionally: citizenship verification for **federal** registrants; alternative-evidence path with **anti-chill** for officials; published match criteria; fee waivers / name-change procedures; dual systems so state races are not nationalized; transparency/custody floors that answer audit-scope questions without stolen-election rhetoric.

### Open questions

1. Exact cloture vote tallies and amendment text for each March–August 2026 roll call—pull official Senate vote XML before any scorecard product.  
2. Full questionnaire wording for any poll still marketed as “71% / 80% for SAVE America Act.”  
3. LDA report-level coding: which OpenSecrets H.R. 22 clients lobbied *for* vs *against* (summary tables often omit direction).  
4. Empirical false-exclusion vs false-inclusion rates under SAVE-style documentary rules (not slogan estimates).  
5. Whether a reconciliation-limited citizenship module can survive Byrd Rule / parliamentarian review—**legal/process dig, counsel**.  
6. Net effect of Murkowski-style rural accommodation amendments on Charter scores and passability.
