**Adversarial premise (founder):** Politicians often misstate or omit real drivers. Dig starts from conspiracy/critical claims and hunts **suspicious public data**—not from accepting “he said he lacks 60 votes” or job-title explanations as the end of inquiry. Suspicions are **flags**, not proven corruption. Quid pro quo remains **not established** unless a public-record chain exists.

---

## Plain-language answer (founder)

**Suspicious patterns that survive a harder dig:** scheduling theater (floor debate + cloture known in advance to fail; “mock talking filibuster”); refusal of every path that could enact SAVE with a simple majority (nuke filibuster, real talking-filibuster rules change, reconciliation attach that loses GOP votes); summer calendar favoring funding / other priorities and August recess over indefinite SAVE floor occupation; conference memo coaching “we don’t have the votes” messaging while Trump and Heritage Action demand more aggressive procedure; documented Trump–Thune conflict history that makes capital-burning for Trump’s marquee bill less likely than institutional self-preservation.

**Best-fit non-corruption explanation (still not the press release alone):** Majority Leader incentives—preserve the legislative filibuster, protect vulnerable Republicans, keep calendar control—plus real veto players (unified Democrats; Murkowski on substance; Tillis/Collins/McConnell on rules/reconciliation). That is **institutional politics**, not “honest broker with no choices.”

**Not established:** paid opposition, donor→burial chain, secret policy hostility (Thune’s Yea votes and floor advocacy cut against that), or that personal Trump grudges are the *causal* driver versus conference whip math.

**Falsifiers for H1 “secret opponent”:** repeated floor advocacy + Yea on cloture + Heritage/Trump pressure *against* him for *more* aggressiveness (wrong direction for anti-SAVE paymaster story).

**Falsifiers for pure “just math, no choice” press release:** Leader chose the choice set (no nuke; mock not real talking filibuster; recess; other bills in the sprint). Math is real; the **menu of options he will not open** is also a choice.

## 6. Steelman — Thune / leadership (mechanisms)

1. **53 ≠ 60.** Cloture failed 53–47; Democrats united against; Murkowski opposes substance as written ([Democracy Docket / WSJ framing](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/murkowski-slams-door-shut-on-supporting-save-america-act/); [Tillis statement](https://www.tillis.senate.gov/2026/3/tillis-statement-on-the-save-america-act)).  
2. **Filibuster nuke lacks conference majority.** Tillis and others say they will never vote to eliminate; Thune says votes aren’t there ([ABC](https://abcnews.com/Politics/thune-filibuster-after-trumps-threats-save-america-act/story?id=130906523)). Forcing a doomed rules vote can fracture the majority.  
3. **Talking filibuster without rules change** risks Democratic floor control / endless amendment chaos (Tillis steelman)—not free lunch.  
4. **Vulnerable map.** Midterm campaigning / recess matters for Collins and others ([AP/PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/trump-demands-senate-cancel-august-break-until-it-passes-the-save-america-act)).  
5. **Must govern elsewhere.** DHS funding, debt/budget, nominees—Leader who only runs messaging votes loses the chamber.  
6. **Public Yea + advocacy** is costly if he were a secret opponent—cheaper to soft-pedal or bury in committee.

---

## 7. Steelman — sandbagging / hidden-driver claims (mechanisms)

1. **Only the Leader opens the nuclear / talking-filibuster menu.** Declining those paths *is* the stall even if he says he supports the bill.  
2. **Theater is observable.** Mock filibuster + “put Democrats on record” + conference coaching of the vote-count line look like managing base anger, not maximizing passage probability.  
3. **Trump conflict makes capital burn unlikely.** Burning the filibuster for Trump’s loyalty-test bill is exactly what a historically conflicted, institutionally minded Leader would refuse—consistent with H2+H3 without needing a bribe.  
4. **Priority inversion.** If SAVE were truly #1 for leadership (not just the White House), August recess and crypto/sanctions would not outrank indefinite SAVE floor pressure.  
5. **Honest limit:** Strongest case is **willful procedural conservatism / institutional self-interest**, possibly colored by Trump distrust—not a public filing that anti-SAVE money bought Thune.

## 10. Per-hypothesis grades (detail)

### H1 — Sandbagging / scheduling theater — **Partially supported**

Supported pieces: mock filibuster strategy; pre-commitment that talking-filibuster votes don’t exist; messaging cloture; conference narrative management; summer priority mix.  
Not established: that he secretly opposes the policy or buried a winnable rules majority.  
Contradicted piece: policy-opponent framing given Yea + advocacy.

### H2 — Trump conflict → reluctance — **Partially supported**

Supported: multi-year public conflict record; 2026 open White House–Leader tension over SAVE/filibuster/recess.  
Not established: that personal animus (vs conference filibuster faction + vulnerable seats) is the *decisive* cause of any single scheduling choice.

### H3 — Institutional Majority Leader incentives — **Supported**

Best single public explanation for the **choice set**: keep filibuster, avoid midterm chaos, diversify calendar, use Murkowski/Tillis/Dem math as binding constraints. This is **not** identical to “I have no agency.”

### H4 — Donor / industry conflict with SAVE — **Not established** (Thune burial)

Aggregate lobbyist/finance totals are noisy (A). Direction of organized pressure on Thune is predominantly **pro-passage** (Trump, Lee, Heritage Action). Bill-level opposing LDA ecosystems exist ([influence companion](influence-save-act-senate-stall.md)) but do not chain to Thune’s official acts.

### H5 — Quid pro quo / paid opposition — **Not established**

No indictment, plea, or filing chain payment → Thune act killing SAVE.
