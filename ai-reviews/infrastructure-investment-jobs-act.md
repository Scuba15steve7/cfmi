## Summary (≤ 120 words)

IIJA authorizes and appropriates large sums for highways, transit, broadband, grid, water, and related programs. Formula highway funding can be rules-based; many new competitive grant programs, Buy America expansions, and agency-selected “transformative” projects introduce political allocation and opaque discretion. CFMI does not oppose roads or bridges as such—it flags **privilege, discretion, and barrier** architecture that steers capital by access rather than open competition or equal, published criteria.

## Conflicts with Charter goals

1. **Opaque competitive grants** allocate infrastructure capital via multi-factor agency judgment.  
2. **Domestic preference / Buy America expansions** can function as entry barriers and privileges for favored suppliers.  
3. **Conditional spending** pressures state policy beyond neutral fiscal transfers.  
4. **Incomplete least-coercive reform** — process/permitting clarity is secondary to new spending authority.

## Hard Flags

- [x] Dimension ≤ 1 (E, F)
- [x] Artificial manipulation match (opaque discretion / preference privilege)

1. **Competitive grant programs** with open-ended merit factors and secretarial priority lists.  
2. **Buy America / domestic content preferences** that privilege certain suppliers.  
3. **Program-specific set-asides** not available on equal published terms to all otherwise eligible applicants.

## Proposed alignment changes (open fix language for bipartisan comment)

1. **Shift** new competitive pots into **formula** or closed-ended schedules with objective, published metrics (lane-miles deficient, bridge rating, unserved broadband locations counted by a public map).  
2. **Narrow Buy America** to transparent, time-limited waivers with published cost/availability findings—or replace with equal tariff/tax treatment rather than project-by-project preference fights.  
3. **Require** machine-readable award justifications and denied-application summaries.  
4. **Add** generally applicable federal permitting shot clocks for IIJA-funded projects **without** creating technology- or ideology-based fast lanes.

## Least-Coercive Alternative Sketch

Fund core networks by transparent formulas; publish outcomes; clear permitting for all projects under equal rules—minimize beauty-contest grants.
