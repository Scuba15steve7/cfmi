Mission

CFMI is research-funded AI for legislative analysis: we score current bills against free markets and constitutional limits, publish conflicts with our Charter goals, and provide open alignment and fix language for proposed legislation. We publish sample acts for bipartisan review and comment, work to minimize unintended consequences, and bind every product to Charter principles. AI is a disclosed research aid under published rules—never an authority.

How we work

Score living bills. Publish conflicts. Offer open fix language. Circulate sample acts for bipartisan comment—always under Charter filters. Look past talking points to operative barriers. Deeper digs use hierarchical AI investigation under the same public rubrics, with a human editor before publish.

  1. Score Use research-funded, rules-bound AI to score current legislation against Charter goals.
  2. Publish conflicts Disclose where bill text conflicts with free markets, constitutional limits, or anti-capture filters—and, where public filings allow, disclosed influence trails beside the score.
  3. Fix language Offer open alignment and amendment language for proposed bills; pair every influence map with alternatives.
  4. Sample acts Publish sample legislation for bipartisan review and comment; minimize unintended consequences.
  5. Bind Charter principles govern every score, draft, and comment cycle.

Principles

Non-negotiable filters for every review and draft. Full text in the Charter.

  • Constitutional fidelity — enumerated powers, federalism, separation of powers, property and contract.
  • Free markets — prices, voluntary exchange, and open entry over political allocation.
  • No artificial manipulation — no incumbent subsidies, entry barriers, opaque discretion, or insulating privileges.
  • Minimal intervention — least coercive instrument first.
  • Radical transparency — methods, AI use, funding, and disclosed influence trails public by default.
  • Anti-capture — we must never become a source of new rents.
  • Competitive federalism — never federal commandeering.
