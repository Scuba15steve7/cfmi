The tool

An AI dig into legislation or topics—so people can understand issues, find constitutional and free-market solutions that put people over bureaucracy, and see suspicion flags from public data when something looks off.

What it does

CFMI is research infrastructure, not a black-box lobby. Disclosed, rules-bound AI digs past talking points to operative barriers, power shifts, and public-record influence trails—then publishes educational outputs anyone can read, challenge, or improve.

  1. Understand Name the mechanism: statute, board, fee, franchise, opaque waiver—not the press release.
  2. Solve Surface constitutional, free-market, people-over-bureaucracy options—least-coercive first; no new rents.
  3. Expose Publish suspicion flags and disclosed conflicts from public filings so citizens can scrutinize—labeled by proof level; never private investigation or unverified bribery claims.

How the AI dig works

Deeper work uses a bounded hierarchy: an orchestrator frames the problem; specialist lanes cover text, root cause, disclosed influence, counters, safeguards, and fix language; a human editor accepts or revises before anything is published. High-consensus and viral claims are tested—not treated as authority (example: SAVE Act Senate stall dig). On high-stakes influence digs, AI cross-links public filings at scale into graded patterns. We publish suspicion flags from public data so citizens can scrutinize what looks off—we do not require absolute proof of corruption to flag a pattern. Every material finding gets dual output: the Flag and its Proof-status (suspicion flag · supported conflict of interest disclosed · corruption / quid pro quo only when a public-record chain supports it). We do not amplify unverified theories as proven bribery, and we do not bury flags under a single “not established.” Primary sources and filings come first; secondary explainers stay non-authoritative.

What a dig produces

A published dig aims for a Civic Action Pack—educational research, not a whip list.

  • Diagnosis — operative barrier and power shift in plain language.
  • Charter score — Hard Flags against constitutional limits, free markets, and anti-capture filters.
  • Both sides — steelmanned reform and status-quo cases with linked evidence.
  • Influence leads — public-record trails paired with Hard Flags.
  • Suspicion flags — Flag + Proof-status even when quid pro quo is not established.
  • Fix language — open least-coercive alternatives.
  • Legislator letter — sample constituent template the reader adapts.
  • Social blurb — short shareable summary with source links.

Suggest & improve

Point CFMI at a bill or topic—or improve an existing brief with counterevidence. Suggestions are public. AI screens them against Charter values—not party lines. Interest disclosure is required.

  1. Suggest Open a public issue: a dig request, a review topic, or a correction for an existing brief.
  2. AI screens & ranks Disclosed AI scores Charter fit; a human editor accepts or revises before anything is ranked, declined, or published.
  3. Public queue No private side channel for rents or pay-for-play.

Ground rules

  • Interest disclosure is mandatory. Employment, clients, subsidies, association membership, or other material interests related to the subject in the prior 24 months—or write “None.”
  • Charter filters, not caucus filters. Suggestions from any party or none are welcome under the same rules. Steelman counters are invited—rent asks are not.
  • Anti-capture. Asks for subsidies, entry barriers, opaque discretion, or named privileges are declined and logged.
  • Educational research. Not legal advice, voting instructions, or counsel to any person. Packs and rankings are not a promise of a full review on a deadline. Default formation path is 501(c)(3) educational/research—no tax status claimed.
