Government as bottleneck

Each example pairs a plain-language description with CFMI’s educational approach: score living bills, publish Charter conflicts, offer open fix language, and circulate sample text for bipartisan review—not whip votes. Priority briefs steelman both sides with linked evidence. Full briefs live in the public repository— suggest a correction or counterargument anytime.

  • Occupational licensing

    Entry barriers · state boards

    Safety is the usual rationale; evidence often shows incumbent shelter, thinner entry and mobility, and higher consumer prices—with little quality gain. Some scope rules also route work through licensed intermediaries.

    Supporting reform

    CEA 2015, FTC competition advocacy, and Kleiner/NBER mobility research document price, entry, and interstate barriers; sunrise reviews usually reject new licenses. Affected braiders (IJ client stories) show hour mandates unrelated to the work.

    Counterarguments

    Asymmetric information can justify minimum standards (Leland); boards cite public protection and discipline (FSMB, ANA title protection); some work finds higher willingness to pay for licensed labor (Kleiner & Soltas).

    CFMI response Accept narrow, evidence-tied safety gates where harm is real; reject overbroad hours, incumbent-capture boards, and opaque discretion. Prefer certification, bonding, and objective criteria before full licensure. Score expansions; publish conflicts; circulate least-coercive sample text for bipartisan comment.

    Sources CEA 2015 · FTC · NBER w24107 · IJ sunrise · Affected (IJ) · Leland 1979 · ANA

  • Housing zoning & permitting

    Hidden barriers · land use · process

    Willing buyers and builders meet more than “zoning bad.” Buried mechanisms—exclusionary lot sizes, soft “character” vetoes, permit delay, parking minimums, impact fees, overlay freezes, HOA stacks, and supply-taxing “affordability” rules—often do the real work while talking points dominate hearings.

    Supporting reform

    Glaeser/Gyourko document a regulatory price–cost wedge; Hsieh/Moretti tie tight supply in productive cities to national misallocation. California YIMBY / Terner evidence shows state upzoning can fail when local fees and design rules undercut it. Parking minimums and inclusionary taxes raise the cost of new units.

    Counterarguments

    APA and local governments steelman use separation, nexus-tied impact fees, infrastructure concurrency, and home-rule flexibility. Environmental review can surface real harms; not every delay is capture. Deed-restricted units help specific households even when supply taxes are contested.

    CFMI response Accept objective safety and capacity rules with metrics; reject soft character vetoes, delay-as-veto, and affordability politics that tax supply. Prefer by-right missing-middle, shot clocks, and published standards. Score bills for buried barriers; circulate least-coercive sample text for bipartisan comment.

    Sources Glaeser/Gyourko JEP · Hsieh/Moretti · CA YIMBY SB 9 · APA impact fees · Affected (IJ)

  • Election administration integrity

    Constitutional process · transparency · anti-corruption

    Voter-list accuracy, mail-ballot custody, and citizenship verification (including SAVE Act debates) are often replaced by slogans—“fully secure” or “stolen”—that skip threat models, audit scope, and state rules. In scope for CFMI as constitutional transparency, not as a free-market flagship.

    Supporting stronger verification

    Heritage and list-maintenance advocates argue citizenship is required but often unverified; GAO documents hard, incomplete roll cleanup; CISA’s own mail-voting risk materials note registration-integrity exposure—not zero residual risk.

    Counterarguments

    CISA/NASS and MIT Election Lab stress layered safeguards and generally functional voter experience; Brennan Center and voting-rights groups steelman SAVE Act burdens on citizens without ready passport or birth certificate and argue noncitizen voting is vanishingly rare.

    CFMI response Mechanism analysis only—no vibe reassurance, no vibe alarm. Define “secure,” separate error from fraud, score bills like SAVE Act on least-coercive verification plus access safeguards; oppose security theater and anti-scrutiny narratives alike.

    Sources H.R. 22 SAVE Act · CISA mail risk · GAO-19-485 · Heritage Action · Brennan Center · MIT Election Lab
