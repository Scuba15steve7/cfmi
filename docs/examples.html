Examples

Where government—not open competition—is the obstacle. CFMI’s research mission is to find these issues in living law, score the bills that entrench them, and invite bipartisan comment on open fix language.

Government as bottleneck

Each example is a short issue brief: the problem in plain language, how statute or regulation blocks market solutions, which Charter principles apply, and what a CFMI-style AI product would look for. Full briefs live in the public repository.

  • Occupational licensing

    Entry barriers · state boards

    Lawful work often requires a government permission slip written and enforced with incumbent input. Safety needs clear rules; much licensing goes far beyond that and raises rivals’ costs.

    Read the brief

  • Housing zoning & permitting

    Land use · discretionary process

    Willing buyers and builders meet zoning bans, design review, and multi-year permits. Supply cannot legally respond; scarcity is largely artificial.

    Read the brief

  • Certificate of Need

    Healthcare capacity monopolies

    In CON states, new hospitals and facilities need political approval before competing. Patients lose choice; capital ready to serve is blocked by statute.

    Read the brief

  • Western water transfers

    Property rights · opaque vetoes

    Scarce water needs clear, transferable rights. Vague “public interest” reviews and anti-transfer defaults freeze reallocation that voluntary exchange could achieve.

    Read the brief

  • Accreditation gatekeeping

    Credential cartels

    When government makes one accreditor or school pipeline the only lawful path to aid or licensure, alternatives—apprenticeship, competency exams—are shut out.

    Read the brief

  • Industrial subsidies & privileges

    Incumbent shelter · political allocation

    Durable subsidies, loan guarantees, and named preferences tilt capital toward political access. Open competition is the casualty—not a missing market.

    Read the brief

  • Opaque discretionary permitting

    Waivers · process vetoes

    Projects stall under “as appropriate” and “public interest” authority without published criteria or deadlines. Government process—not demand—binds the outcome.

    Read the brief

  • Exclusive franchises & closed AI rules

    Legal monopolies · tech entry barriers

    Exclusive utility franchises and emerging AI licensing or favored-vendor rules can repeat the same pattern: statute picks who may serve.

    Read the brief
