Suggest

Point CFMI at a bill or topic—or improve an existing brief with counterevidence. Suggestions are public. AI screens them against Charter values—not party lines. Interest disclosure is required.

How it works

Zero paid intake. GitHub Issues keep suggestions versioned and visible—radical transparency by default.

  1. Suggest Open a public issue: a new review topic, or a correction / counterargument for an existing brief. Interest disclosure is mandatory either way.
  2. AI screens & ranks Disclosed, rules-bound AI scores fit against Charter principles and Methodology weights—or routes counterevidence to the matching brief. A human editor accepts or revises before anything is published as ranked, declined, or merged into a brief.
  3. Public queue Review suggestions land in the open queue—Pending, Ranked, or Declined. Counterevidence updates appear in the brief’s Voices & evidence sections when accepted. No private side channel for rents or pay-for-play.

Counterevidence & brief improvement

Issue briefs must steelman both sides so reforms stay passable and effective. If we missed a paper, a professional-association safety case, or an affected-people profile, send it.

  • Use the counterevidence template: issue slug, link, which side, what CFMI should change, interest disclosure.
  • Supporting reform, defense of the status quo, affected-people sources, and factual corrections are all in scope.
  • Accepted evidence updates the brief’s Voices & evidence tables and, when material, the CFMI response or “what would make reform passable” section.
  • Exemplar brief: occupational licensing · Template: _brief-template.md.

Ground rules

  • Interest disclosure is mandatory. Employment, clients, subsidies, association membership, or other material interests related to the subject in the prior 24 months—or write “None.”
  • Charter filters, not caucus filters. Suggestions from any party or none are welcome under the same rules. Steelman counters are invited—rent asks are not.
  • Anti-capture. Asks for subsidies, entry barriers, opaque discretion, or named privileges are declined and logged.
  • Educational research. Not legal advice, voting instructions, or counsel to any person. Ranking is not a promise of a full review on a deadline.
